# Implementation Log — More Verticality, Remaining Clip Fixes, Tiered Lift

Follow-up to [08_006.md](08_006.md). `tools/run_tests.ps1`: 50/50 passing,
playability sim confirmed reliable across 3 consecutive runs.

## Verticality increased substantially

- Height range: `MAX_CENTER_Y` 1.8 → 3.2 (nearly 2x the climbable band).
- Slope angle distribution: was heavily biased toward the low end
  (`pow(rng.randf(), 3)`), now much flatter (`pow(rng.randf(), 1.3)`) - the
  full 5-45° range shows up regularly, not just occasionally.
- `MAX_STEP_HEIGHT_ABS` (the per-step climb/descend cap) raised, then
  dialed back once during tuning to keep the harder course provably
  beatable - landed at 0.28 (up from 0.2).

## Meshes were still clipping - two real remaining sources found

1. **Bridge slope math used the wrong points.** The connecting plank's tilt
   was computed from each piece's *center* position, but its vertical
   placement used the *top-surface* midpoint - fine when both pieces were
   the same thickness, wrong whenever they weren't (a thin Ruler next to a
   chunky ToyBlock, which is most of the time). The plank could end up
   angled differently than the true top-to-top slope and dip below the
   taller piece's actual surface - clipping into its side. Fixed to build
   the plank between the two real top-surface points throughout.
2. **Tilted visual meshes were poking past their own collision box.** Blocks
   touch their neighbors with *exactly* zero gap - any rotation of the
   visual mesh without also shrinking it will push a corner past that seam
   into the neighbor, regardless of how small the tilt config allowed for.
   Cosmetic tilt range roughly halved (yaw ±12°→±5°, pitch/roll ±6°→±3°),
   and the mesh itself is now uniformly scaled to 93% as a second margin on
   top of that.

## "The last section leads inside other geometry"

This was the room's fixed furniture (table, bed, CRT TV), not the course
pieces themselves - `CoursePath` has no idea where they are, and the
side-to-side steering that was supposed to keep the winding course inward
was a soft *nudge* (`lerp_angle` blend) that could still overshoot past a
piece of furniture before it corrected. Two fixes: the steering is now a
hard override the instant the course crosses the bound (not a blend), and
the furniture itself was moved further out (table/bed to z=8.3, CRT TV to
z=-9, right at the edge the room's walls allow). Added a permanent
regression test (`generated course stays clear of the room's fixed
furniture`) - the exact category of thing that's easy to reintroduce
without a human noticing when tuning course-generation numbers later.

## Whacker vertical lift, explicitly tiered

Putter stayed at "little to none" (0.02 - already there). 5 Iron nudged to
"medium" (0.16 → 0.18). Driver to "heavy" (0.26 → 0.34, on top of its
already-highest power) - it's now clearly the club for clearing a jump gap
or a steep slope, not just the hardest flat hit.

## The playability sim needed real work to keep up

More verticality + real jump gaps meant more ways to get stuck, and one of
them was a genuine bug in the test itself worth recording: the sim's
jump-detection had a `not near_goal and ...` condition, meaning **a
mandatory jump gap landing on the very last piece was never detected as a
jump** (gap placement doesn't know or care where the goal ends up) - the
final approach used regular rolling power/loft against what was actually a
gap it could never cross, and got stuck retrying forever. This produced a
long, expensive debugging chase (several rounds of "increase power/loft
globally", which didn't fix a bug that was really about a missing case, not
insufficient force) before the actual root cause turned up. Fixed by
checking `has_gap_before` unconditionally, regardless of whether the target
happens to be the goal itself. Worth remembering: a stuck-forever pattern in
this sim is more often a missing case in the sim's own decision logic than
"the course needs to be easier."
