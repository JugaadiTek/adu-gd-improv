## Bug: hitting the eraser backward didn't reset

Reproduced headlessly (strike hard toward -X off the start): the ball came
to rest fully, geometrically overlapping `FloorSensor`'s Area3D volume -
same layer/mask, `monitoring = true` - and just sat there forever.
`get_overlapping_bodies()` confirmed it: genuinely never detected, not a
one-frame tunneling fluke. Root cause in the physics/Area3D interaction
wasn't worth chasing further once a strictly more reliable mechanism was
available: **`Eraser` now checks its own Y position every physics frame**
(`lava_y_threshold = 0.15`, just above the floor's actual resting height and
comfortably below any legitimate on-course position) instead of depending on
the signal at all. `FloorSensor` is still there but is no longer the
authoritative mechanism. Regression-tested (strike backward, confirm it
returns to start).

## "DO NOT OVERLAP MESHES ON THE COURSE"

Bridge planks were resting up to 0.7m onto each block's top (deliberately,
for a flush-looking connection) - reasonable in isolation, but conflicts
with a hard "no overlapping meshes" rule once stated. Flipped the sign:
`BRIDGE_INSET = 0.1` now pulls each plank slightly *short* of both blocks
instead of overlapping either one. Trade-off stated plainly: there's a
hairline seam at each end now rather than a flush rest.

## Verticality: real slopes, not just small steps

Height changes are now **held slope runs**: every 2-4 pieces a target angle
in **5-45°** is picked (biased toward the lower end - `pow(rng.randf(), 3)` -
so 45° moments are real but occasional, not the norm) and several
consecutive pieces climb or descend together at that one grade, capped at
0.3m of height change per single step regardless of angle (a 45° slope over
a long step would otherwise demand an unplayable one-hop climb - the ramp
across a held run is still a genuine 5-45° slope, just spread over more than
one piece when the angle is steep).

## Mandatory jump gaps (2-3, no bridge)

`CoursePath` now places real air gaps - no touching, no bridge - at
roughly 35/60/85% of the path length, guarded so two can never land
back-to-back (a real player needs a landing spot to line up the next shot).
`GAP_DISTANCE = 1.1m`. `LevelController` skips the bridge for any piece
marked `has_gap_before`. The playability sim's simulated player treats these
specially - full driver power + steep loft (0.4) - the way an actual player
lining up a mandatory jump would swing, rather than the usual
distance-scaled guess (which was calibrated for rolling to a stop near a
target, not clearing a gap - using it for gaps was the difference between
"reaches checkpoint 1" and "stuck forever re-attempting the same failed jump
from the same respawn point", which is exactly what the sim caught before
this fix).

## Driver: more lift

`loft_factor` 0.08 → 0.26 (now higher than the 5 Iron's 0.16, not lower -
explicitly requested, not "realistic golf" driver behavior).

## Lava floor: real animated shader

Replaced the script-driven emission pulse with `shaders/lava_floor.gdshader`
(native Godot shader, as asked) - two layers of cheap hash-based value noise
at different scroll speeds/directions, one distorting the other, driving
both the albedo gradient (dark → mid → hot) and emission strength. No
textures needed; it's procedural. `lava_floor.gd` (the old script) is gone.
